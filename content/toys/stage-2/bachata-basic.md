## What you're listening for

Bachata groups time into a four-beat phrase. You count "1-2-3-tap." The tap on beat 4 is not a full step: it is a weight touch, a micro-pause, and a hip accent all at once. When you hit the tap with the hip pop, you are doing what the drums, bass, and guitar are doing in the music at that exact moment. The body and the band answer each other.

## How to read the code

Optional Strudel patch below. It plays a slow bass-and-clap groove at 120 bpm to give you a steady reference for the four-beat phrase.

```strudel
// 4-beat slow groove for practising the bachata 1-2-3-tap.
s("bd*4, [~ ~ ~ cp]*1").bank("RolandTR909").cpm(120)
```

The `bd*4` is four even kicks (your beats 1-2-3-4). The `cp` on the fourth beat of each bar marks where the tap happens in your body.

## Try it

**Say the count first:**

1. Say "1, 2, 3, tap" at a slow, steady pace. The "tap" replaces the word "four." When you say "tap," let a small accent come into your voice. That accent is the hip pop.

**Now add feet and hips (side-to-side basic):**

First four-beat phrase (stepping right):
- Beat 1: step your **right foot to the right**. Weight fully onto right foot.
- Beat 2: step your **left foot** to close toward the right (or small step right). Weight to left.
- Beat 3: step your **right foot** one more step to the right. Weight to right.
- Beat 4 (tap): touch your **left foot beside your right foot**, no full weight transfer. As you touch, let your **right hip pop up slightly**. This is the accent.

Second four-beat phrase (stepping left):
- Beat 1: step your **left foot to the left**. Weight fully onto left foot.
- Beat 2: step your **right foot** to close toward the left. Weight to right.
- Beat 3: step your **left foot** one more step to the left. Weight to left.
- Beat 4 (tap): touch your **right foot beside your left foot**, no full weight transfer. As you touch, let your **left hip pop up slightly**.

**The hip pop comes from the standing leg, not the touching leg.** When you tap your left foot on beat 4 and you are standing on your right leg, the right hip rises. Think of it as the standing hip "catching" the tap beat.

**Run the patch or find a bachata track (try Aventura's "Obsesion" or Prince Royce's "Stand By Me" cover at around 120-130 bpm).** Do the step. Count out loud. Let the hip movement grow slowly as the step becomes familiar.

## In the world

Bachata is a close-hold partner dance. The basic side-to-side step you learned is the foundation every turn, dip, and figure is built on. In partner dancing, the leader and follower mirror each other: when the leader steps right, the follower steps left in the same count. The hip pop happens on the same beat 4 in both bodies simultaneously. When it clicks, both people feel it land together.

Modern bachata sensual (developed in Cali, Colombia, and Spain from around 2005 onward) uses body waves and tilts on top of the same four-beat phrase. The count and the hip accent remain the same.

## Music theory note

Bachata is in 4/4 time. Unlike salsa, it does not use the clave as an organizing timeline. The rhythm section in a bachata band is built around a specific guitar pattern called the **requinto** (a high melodic guitar) and a rhythm guitar playing a steady pattern, plus bongos and bass. The bass tends to follow a syncopated pattern that emphasizes beats 1 and 3, making the 4 feel like a suspension before the phrase restarts.

The connection to clave-based traditions is indirect. Dominican music absorbed African rhythmic patterns through a different cultural path than Cuban music. The syncopation in bachata comes partly from that shared African heritage but arrived via merengue, bolero, and son influences rather than directly via the clave family. When you later study the son clave in stage 3, you will be able to hear what bachata shares and what it does differently.

## History

Bachata began in the rural and working-class neighborhoods of the Dominican Republic in the 1960s. The guitar-based style was stigmatized by the urban middle class and kept off mainstream radio for decades. The first bachata recording is attributed to **Jose Manuel Calderon**, who released "Borracho de amor" in 1962. The music circulated in small bars (bares de mala muerte, roughly "dive bars") and through word of mouth.

In the 1990s, artists like **Antony Santos** brought bachata to wider Dominican audiences and to the diaspora in the United States and Spain. The music changed: productions became more polished, tempos slowed slightly, and the romantic lyrical themes became more prominent.

**Romeo Santos** (of Aventura, formed in the Bronx in 1994) took bachata global in the 2000s and 2010s with productions that crossed into pop and R&B. **Prince Royce** (also from the Bronx) followed a similar path. By the 2010s bachata was played in salsa clubs worldwide. The dance form that developed alongside the music is now one of the most widely taught Latin social dances, with bachata sensual variants developed in Spain and Colombia adding new movement vocabularies.

## Dance and body

Stand still. Touch your left foot to the floor beside your right foot, but put almost no weight on it. Just a touch.

Now let your right hip rise slightly as you do that touch. The hip pop is small. It is not a big theatrical movement. It is more like a sigh in the body: a brief release of weight.

Do this ten times in place: touch left, right hip pops, touch left, right hip pops. Keep the upper body still. The movement lives from the waist down.

Now add the step: right-right-right-tap (right hip), left-left-left-tap (left hip). When the hip pop is happening without you thinking about it, find a bachata track and let the music set the pace.

The body goal: the tap and the hip pop arrive together, on the same beat the music accents. When that happens, you are not dancing to the music. You are part of it.

## Self-test

Find any bachata track. Start the basic step without counting. After two or three phrases, ask yourself: is the hip pop landing on the same beat as the accent in the music? If not, slow down and count out loud until it does.
