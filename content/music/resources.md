# Resources: the tutorial pile

Free and open-license tutorials, sorted by topic. Gathered by research agents and
verified. Open `learning-journey.md` for the path that uses them.

## Legend

- **[OPEN]** = openly licensed (Creative Commons or open-source). You may reuse and adapt it.
- **[FREE]** = free to use, but copyrighted. Learn from it, do not copy/republish it.
- **[PAID]** = part is free, part is behind a paywall. Flagged so you do not hit a wall.
- ⭐ = best fit for you (beginner, plain language, visual or interactive).

A note on "open": most of the *best beginner tools* (Ableton, ear trainers) are **free but
not open**. The genuinely open ones (reusable) are Wikipedia, Open Music Theory, and the
software itself (Strudel, Mixxx). Both are fine to learn from. The label just tells you
what you may legally copy.

## 4. House music: beats, structure, history

- **[OPEN] Wikipedia: House music** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_music — CC BY-SA. Definition, the drum pattern, history, subgenres. Key facts: 120 to 130 BPM; kick on every beat (four-on-the-floor); open hi-hat on the offbeats; clap/snare on beats 2 and 4.
- **[OPEN] Wikipedia: Styles of house music** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styles_of_house_music — CC BY-SA. The family tree (deep, tech, progressive, acid, Afro, Latin...).
- **[OPEN] Wikipedia: The Warehouse (nightclub)** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warehouse_(nightclub) — CC BY-SA. Where the name "house" comes from; Frankie Knuckles.
- **[FREE] Four-on-the-floor explained** — https://www.masterclass.com/articles/four-on-the-floor-rhythm-explained
- **[FREE] How to make a house beat** — https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-a-house-beat — the layer stack, plain language.
- **[FREE] EDM song structure** (intro/build/drop/breakdown/outro, with bar counts) — https://edmtips.com/edm-song-structure/
- **[FREE] NPR: birth of house music** — https://www.npr.org/2023/03/02/1160484070/chicago-birth-of-house-music

**The beat map (memorise this):** in one bar of 4 beats: kick on **1, 2, 3, 4**; clap/snare on **2 and 4**; open hi-hat on the **offbeats** (between the kicks). Tempo ~120 to 128 BPM.

## 7. Afro and Latin music + dancing + history

This is your motivation anchor. House and your favourite Afro/Latin music are the same
family tree: West African bell → Cuban clave → jazz and disco → Black/Latino/queer clubs
in Chicago and NY → house → Afro house, Amapiano.

### The rhythms (with grids you can code)

`x` = hit, `.` = rest. In Strudel use a sound name for `x` and `~` for `.`.

- **Tresillo** (the DNA cell, 3+3+2): `x . . x . . x .` → `s("cp ~ ~ cp ~ ~ cp ~")`
- **Son clave 3-2**: `x . . x . . x . . . x . x . . .` → `s("cp ~ ~ cp ~ ~ cp ~ ~ ~ cp ~ cp ~ ~ ~")`
- **Rumba clave 3-2** (3rd hit slides one step): `x . . x . . . x . . x . x . . .`
- **12/8 West African bell** (the ancestor): `x . x . x x . x . x . x` → `s("cp ~ cp ~ cp cp ~ cp ~ cp ~ cp")`
- **3-against-2 polyrhythm**: `stack(s("hh*3"), s("bd*2"))`

### Dancing (the method, not a bonus)

Moving to a beat is a real brain process (auditory-motor entrainment) that builds your
internal pulse, and it helps AuDHD focus. The home routine: put on a track you love, soften
your knees, bounce once per beat, count "1-2-3-4" out loud. One move per session.

## 8. How this journey is built (the teaching research)

The journey is designed on evidence, not guesswork. Strongest-evidence ideas first.

- **[OPEN] Cognitive Load Theory** (chunk it, one thing at a time) — NSW gov report: https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/about-us/educational-data/cese/2017-cognitive-load-theory.pdf
- **Retrieval practice + spacing** (test yourself; review on a 1-3-7-14-30 day ladder) — Cepeda meta-analysis: https://augmentingcognition.com/assets/Cepeda2006.pdf
- **Self-regulated learning** (goal, monitor, reflect) — Zimmerman: https://www.leiderschapsdomeinen.nl/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/Zimmerman-B.-2002-Becoming-Self-Regulated-Learner.pdf
- **Scaffolding / gradual release** (I do, we do, you do) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradual_release_of_responsibility
- **[OPEN] Universal Design for Learning** (multiple ways in) — CAST: https://udlguidelines.cast.org (check exact license before any commercial reuse)
- **Monotropism** (autistic attention tunnels; why special interest is fuel) — https://www.autism.org.uk/advice-and-guidance/professional-practice/what-is-monotropism and https://monotropism.org/murray-lesser-lawson/
- **Implementation intentions** (if-then start cues beat task-initiation freeze) — Gollwitzer & Sheeran meta-analysis.
- **Andragogy** (adults need the "why" and immediate use) — https://infed.org/dir/welcome/andragogy-what-is-it-and-does-it-help-thinking-about-adult-learning/
- **Sound before symbol** (hear it, then name it) — https://pianodao.com/2015/10/17/sound-before-symbol-lessons-from-history/
- **Teach Like a Champion 3.0** (2021, current; borrowed: Check for Understanding, Right is Right, No Opt Out, small-rep practice) — https://teachlikeachampion.org/books/teach-like-a-champion-3-0/

**Honesty on evidence strength:**
- **Strong:** cognitive load, retrieval practice, spacing, multimedia (words + visuals), if-then planning.
- **Solid framework, modest proof:** self-regulation, scaffolding, deliberate-practice parts, UDL.
- **Useful theory, lighter proof:** monotropism, special-interest learning, andragogy, and the AuDHD "stable shell + novel content" pattern.

We lean hardest on the strong ones and treat the rest as sensible design guides.
